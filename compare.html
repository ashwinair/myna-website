Myna vs Otter, Granola, Fireflies and anarlog

I make Myna, so read this knowing that. I've tried to be fair: each section says plainly where the other app is the better choice.

The short version: Otter, Fireflies and Granola are cloud services with more features. anarlog is free and open source, for people happy to configure it. Myna is for people who want meeting notes on a Mac that never leave the Mac, with nothing to set up.

Myna vs Otter

Otter is the best-known name here. Its meeting assistant joins your Zoom, Meet or Teams call as a participant, records it and processes everything on Otter's servers. It now also has a desktop app that records without a bot, but the audio still goes to the cloud.

Choose Otter if

  • You need each speaker identified by name
  • You want notes on your phone or in a browser
  • Your team shares and searches meetings together

Choose Myna if

  • You don't want a bot announcing itself in your calls
  • You don't want recordings on anyone's servers
  • You want to pay less than half the price

Myna vs Fireflies

Fireflies works much like Otter: a notetaker joins your calls, and recording, transcription and search happen in its cloud. It's built for teams, with many integrations into CRMs and work tools.

Choose Fireflies if

  • You want notes sent automatically into your CRM or project tools
  • You need speaker names and team-wide search
  • You're on Windows or work mostly in a browser

Choose Myna if

  • Your calls are confidential and shouldn't leave your computer
  • You'd rather not create an account or connect your calendar to a service
  • You only need notes for yourself, on your Mac

Myna vs Granola

Granola is the closest in spirit: no bot, it records from your computer, and it's pleasant to use. The difference is where the work happens. Granola sends your audio to the cloud to be transcribed (it says the audio is deleted afterwards), and keeps your transcripts and notes on its servers.

Choose Granola if

  • You want the strongest summaries for long meetings, from large cloud AI models
  • You use Windows or want an iPhone app
  • You want to share notes with your team

Choose Myna if

  • You want transcripts and notes that never leave your Mac
  • You want it to work on a plane or with Wi-Fi off
  • You don't want an account

Myna vs anarlog

anarlog (formerly Hyprnote) is a free, open-source meeting notepad with a lot of flexibility. You choose how it transcribes and summarizes: on your computer, through your own AI provider, or through its paid cloud.

Choose anarlog if

  • You want open-source software you can inspect or build yourself
  • You're on Windows or Linux
  • You like choosing your own AI models and don't mind setting them up

Choose Myna if

  • You want it to work out of the box, with nothing to configure
  • You want an app reviewed and distributed by Apple, with billing through the App Store
  • You want no cloud option at all, so nothing can be sent by mistake

Where Myna falls short

  • It only runs on Macs with Apple Silicon and macOS 26. Summaries also need Apple Intelligence turned on.
  • Summaries come from Apple's on-device model, which is smaller than the cloud models others use. They're good for everyday meetings; very long meetings are summarized in parts.
  • The transcript separates you from everyone else, but doesn't name each speaker.
  • There's no phone app, no team sharing and no integrations with other tools.
